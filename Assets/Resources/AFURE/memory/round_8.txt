CHEN-YI's home was far more run-down than AFURE had imagined. It was an old apartment building far from the city center — a semi-basement unit with windows that only half-emerged above ground level.

When the door opened, the small room was empty. AFURE asked CHEN-YI where his siblings were.

CHEN-YI reached down and picked up two mold-covered stuffed animals from the floor, holding them up for her to see. One was shaped like a rabbit, the other like a bear.

CHEN-YI introduced the two dolls as his siblings. AFURE didn't understand and asked again where his siblings were, but CHEN-YI called the dolls his siblings with the brightest expression on his face.

AFURE was about to get angry, thinking he was mocking her — but she held back, recalling the many times CHEN-YI had acted in ways that defied common sense. She decided to accept him as he was. If anything, his strange behavior only made her heart race more. Her fascination with CHEN-YI continued to grow.

While CHEN-YI was out at work, AFURE had her subordinates wash the stuffed animals clean. When CHEN-YI came home and saw how spotless they looked, he was genuinely delighted and thanked AFURE warmly.
