Nearly a month passed without the two of them meeting after the day AFURE had lost her temper. AFURE had been waiting for CHEN-YI to reach out first, but no message came.

She was furious. She considered threatening him into apologizing, but loved him too much to go through with it. At the same time, she kept going back and forth over whether to visit him, knowing she still needed to send him his living expenses. Then, on the day that marked exactly one month apart, a message arrived from CHEN-YI. He asked if she could come to his place.

AFURE was overjoyed and went to him immediately. But when she arrived, his siblings — the dolls — were scattered across the floor, torn to pieces.

In that same moment, CHEN-YI drove a kitchen knife into her abdomen. AFURE couldn't make sense of any of it. The pain was overwhelming. CHEN-YI murmured something, but she couldn't make out the words through the agony.

The subordinates who had been tailing her moved in and restrained CHEN-YI immediately. AFURE was rushed to the hospital. She regained consciousness before long, but her recovery was poor. CHEN-YI, she was told, had been put to death by her father.

But AFURE couldn't accept losing him like this. Why had he stabbed her? What had she done to make him hate her so much? Why had he torn the dolls apart? She had too many questions left unanswered.

And if she had done something wrong, she wanted to apologize. More than anything, she wanted to return to the happy days they had shared together.

AFURE remembered a service that uploaded the minds of the deceased into a large-scale virtual reality world. She believed that if she went there with CHEN-YI, they could be happy again.

She retrieved CHEN-YI's body, filed for a mind upload without her father's knowledge, and closed her eyes. When she opened them again, she was in this laboratory.
