Looking at AFURE made him uneasy. He found himself watching her expressions carefully. The memory of those moneylender's men who used to shadow her came back to him. Of course, as the boss's daughter, being protected was only natural. He told himself that associating with someone like him — a debtor — was probably little more than charity work on her part.

And yet CHEN-YI liked AFURE. She had given him so much. But he was afraid of growing accustomed to this happiness. Afraid of enjoying something that didn't feel like his to have. The fact that she brought him happiness without any conditions whatsoever — it frightened him. Made him suspicious.

He remembered the day they first kissed. AFURE and CHEN-YI had told each other they were in love. And CHEN-YI had gone ahead and said it too — presumptuous as it was.

There had been a time when CHEN-YI was gravely ill. Shortly after his parents first left and he found himself buried under their debts, he had apparently concluded that the only things a child his age could sell were his own organs. He handed over his right eye and a kidney to the moneylenders. For a while he managed, but gradually his body began to fail him.

There was no money for a hospital. He couldn't leave his young siblings alone at home for long stretches of time. There was simply nothing he could do. His siblings who knew nothing of the world, and him lying sick in bed — was this really the right way to live?

That was the first day CHEN-YI regretted his sacrifice.
