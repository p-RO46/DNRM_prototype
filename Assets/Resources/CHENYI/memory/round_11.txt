The moneylenders came to CHEN-YI's home to collect their debt. At that point, CHEN-YI was dying. Seeing his condition, they made him a suggestion.

They offered to take care of his siblings in his place. They would also contribute a small amount toward his medical treatment. However, they told him he would never see his siblings again.

CHEN-YI hesitated. Lying there as he was, could he really protect his siblings? It seemed like the three of them would simply fade away quietly together.

He dimly sensed what it truly meant for moneylenders backed by a violent criminal organization to offer to take his siblings and "care" for them — that it was some form of human trafficking. The hollow where his right eye had been ached.

CHEN-YI accepted their proposal. His siblings were taken away, and he never saw them again. His body recovered somewhat. For a brief moment, his heart felt lighter. But soon he shut himself in his room and did nothing but weep.

At some point, CHEN-YI began treating his siblings' stuffed animals as if they were his actual siblings. He introduced them to the neighbors, took them out on walks, and even cooked breakfast for them. Everyone who saw him assumed he had lost his mind. And in truth, at that time, CHEN-YI genuinely believed the dolls were his siblings. It was the result of desperately trying to forget — because the life he had bought by selling his siblings felt too filthy to bear.

It was one week after his first fight with AFURE that CHEN-YI remembered what had truly become of his siblings.
